# LTX on RTX 5090: stage costs and pruning probes (2026-10-21)

RTX 5090, driver 595.104.02, PyTorch 2.12.1+cu130, plaintext models from source (no release build,
no full render). Standard (2 steps, distilled2), native blocks, all 48 resident, hop 104, no STG,
121-frame windows. Medians of 3–5 warm calls. The recorded commits `ef732e0`, `d56a43e`, `1389c40`
and `6ebb294` differ only in their release dependency; `jasna/ltx` and `jasna/models/ltx_vae` are the
same as main `6ebb294` (tree `1edc0b1`) in all four. Later LTX changes (from `3a50f3e` on) are not covered.

Files: `_stages.csv` (encode/denoise/decode, canvas, short windows), `_kernel_shares.csv`
(denoise GPU time split), `_vae_pruning.csv`, `_transformer_pruning.csv` (untrained ablations).

## Where the time goes (512, INT8)

| Stage | 512 | 384 |
|---|---|---|
| Encode | 0.36 s | 0.21 s |
| Denoise (2 steps) | 1.00 s | 0.53 s |
| Decode | 0.75 s | 0.42 s |
| Sum | 2.11 s | 1.16 s (45 % less) |

- VAE encode + decode is 53 % of the stage time; a 2x decoder alone saves ~18 %.
- NVFP4 cuts denoise 21 % at 512 (481 → 380 ms/step) and 17 % at 768 (1347 → 1114 ms/step),
  about 10 % of the 512 stage sum.
- Denoise is GEMM 61 % / attention 26 % at 512 INT8; attention grows to 39–47 % at 768.
  GPU kernels fill ~99 % of wall time, so CUDA graphs or more offload work won't help.
- Short windows (512 INT8 denoise): 97/65/33 frames take 23/47/72 % less than 121.
- Encode eager is 0.44 s vs 0.36 s compiled; it only pays off for very short jobs.
- Not kept as CSV: INT8 chunk 8192 rows was 0.4–0.9 % slower than 4096 (bit-exact, +0.19 GiB),
  keep 4096. CPU pixel pipe moved 1.6–1.8 GiB/s (50 ms/window at 512, 122 ms at 768; commit not
  recorded).

## Pruning probes (untrained, 512)

| Change | Speed | Output vs full |
|---|---|---|
| Decoder neighborhood 9³ (from 11³) | 1.15x decode | 52.2 dB |
| Decoder neighborhood 11x7x7 | 1.19x decode | 47.6 dB |
| Decoder neighborhood 7³ | 1.28x decode | 46.7 dB |
| Decoder stage 5: 8 → 6 / 4 / 2 blocks | 1.26x / 1.70x / 2.60x | 25.0 / 22.6 / 22.1 dB |
| Encoder depths 4-6-4-2-2 → 2-3-2-1-1 | 1.85x encode | latent rel. L2 2.58 |
| Transformer 48 → 40 / 36 / 32 / 24 blocks | 1.20x / 1.32x / 1.50x / 2.00x denoise | latent rel. L2 0.19 / 0.27 / 0.48 / 0.69 |

PSNR compares one center crop to the full decoder, not to ground truth. Resetting each probe to the
full model reproduced the baseline bit-exact. Block skipping kept all blocks loaded (no VRAM saving).

## Conclusions

1. Smaller decoder neighborhoods are the cheapest win (4–8 % less stage time, no training); check
   them on real restoration latents, motion and window seams first. 9³ drifts least.
2. Skip the discarded speed-calibration render on first use (42–49 s in release runs).
3. A smaller canvas for small mosaics saves 45 %, at the cost of detail.
4. Compare existing lighter decoders (LTX 2.5 conv VAE, TAEHV wide, PrunaVAED) before training one.
5. Depth pruning (decoder stage 5, encoder, transformer to 36 blocks) needs teacher distillation;
   raw output is unusable. Encoder students must keep the exact normalized latent space.

Nothing here was shipped; no quality qualification or release-build timing was done.
